**(a) Feedback on the current method**

**What the record supports**

- **Plan turns with substantive answers.** The Opus plan challenged ending a run at the first turn end; the delegator accepted the change before implementation. That is a concrete design error caught cheaply. Questions about empty-file behaviour, permissions and verification also changed the work.
- **Separate permission to change from permission to investigate and report.** The helper admitted claiming the UI editor could generalise without reading it because the UI was outside its assignment. The distinction addresses an observed failure.
- **Verify what the user experiences.** REST polling passed while websocket activity could disappear entirely. Text assertions passed while the layout jumped. Later geometry assertions passed while the overlay obscured content. Each check established something narrower than the feature needed.
- **Read raw debriefs alongside summaries.** The debrief corrected the delegator’s account of the token implementation and disclosed a knowingly unreported defect. Neither was supplied by the green gate.
- **Put durable facts in documentation.** The poller’s first-sight behaviour and repository naming convention caused avoidable test failures. Repeating those facts in private briefs would leave the next helper exposed.

These are useful findings from particular incidents. They do not prove that every assignment needs the same ceremony.

**What the record does not support**

The three-to-five-feature batch recommendation has no demonstrated optimum behind it. The reported successful initial batch had two features. There is no comparison showing when orientation savings outweigh context growth.

Likewise, “verification is where the hours went in every run” overstates an unmeasured impression. Verification planning helped; the record also shows repeated whole-suite runs after documentation changes or merely because time had passed.

The model recommendations are much stronger than the experiment. Models received different briefs, different evidence, different tasks and different amounts of prior work. The later Opus activity round inherited both an implementation and newly identified stream records. Learning #9 acknowledges the context confound; the models file then speaks as though it has resolved it. “Sonnet designs by editing” describes one observed round. “Seventeen edits where a plan turn would have done” asserts an untested counterfactual.

**What is wrong or missing**

1. **“Shape, not route” is the wrong prohibition.** The successful UI repair brief prescribed positioning, classes, existing components and measurements. Technical direction can help. The failure was prescribing a mechanism without explaining its purpose or exposing its assumptions. Distinguish requirements, verified facts and suggested approaches.

2. **Known defects in the requested work need different treatment from unrelated observations.** The helper noticed that its new sweep could fail to expire a run and withheld it as beyond scope. That compromises the feature being delivered. A “noticed, left alone” section cannot make that acceptable; the reviewer needs it before acceptance.

3. **A report written at completion is not a place to record something “at the moment of noticing.”** The method needs an explicit connection to `am learn`, and consequential discoveries need immediate communication.

4. **The gate has ambiguous ownership.** Helpers run full gates, then the method says the delegator runs the gate itself. Define who owns acceptance and which checks their review requires. Repetition without a changed revision or a new concern is expense, not additional evidence. “Review,” “accepted,” “pushed” and “deployed” should also remain distinct.

5. **The measurement window misses part of the method.** Orientation and plans occur before `in-progress`; debriefs and reviewer work occur after implementation. The run export therefore cannot alone preserve the evidence or total cost needed to judge delegation. Verify preservation before forgetting the helper.

6. **Cause labels are judgments, not causal findings.** `model | brief | doc` can organise reviews, but failures can have several causes, including a changing requirement, environment or inadequate test. The delegator also reviews its own briefing. Require evidence and uncertainty in the note; a mandatory label does not remove either.

7. **Curation is missing from the document about the method.** The learnings feature specifies it, but the method never explains how observations become practice, how contradictions are handled, or how practices are retired. Several initial entries already contain rules or generalisations. All thirteen were entered by one author at 11:27; they are a retrospective collection, not thirteen independent confirmations.

**(b) Proposed complete method document — under 1,500 words**

---

**Working with agents**

This is the current practice for assigning work, reviewing it and learning from it. Repository instructions describe local architecture, checks and deployment. Feature files preserve particular assignments and their decisions. This document describes how those pieces are used together.

The practice is provisional. Its evidence is in the learnings log, feature histories and run records. An incident can justify trying a change without establishing a general rule about agents or models.

**Assigning work**

A feature is a unit of work recorded in `features/<slug>.md`: its purpose, observable result, constraints and history. Read the whole file, including Reports and Responses. Name the latest agreed scope where the history changes the original specification.

A person or delegating agent asks for the work; feature status alone does not queue it. The worker sets `in-progress`, then `review` with a report, or `blocked` with the reason. The reviewer records the response and returns it to `planned` for another round or marks it `done` when accepted.

Name who implements, who reviews and who closes the gate. For work across repositories, identify who owns each companion change and the combined result. There is one writer per repository at a time.

**Choosing whether to delegate**

Delegate when the outcome and relevant context can be communicated well enough for another agent to exercise judgment. Keep work in the existing conversation when essential context or design judgment would be expensive to transfer.

Use `models.md` as a provisional house view. Read its evidence in terms of task, brief, context, model and effort together. A successful later attempt with a better brief does not isolate a model advantage.

Reuse a helper while its context remains useful. Choose batch size according to related work, reviewability and context condition; no fixed optimum has been established.

**Orienting and planning**

A new helper reads the repository instructions, relevant design documentation and assigned feature histories. Its response should identify consequential constraints, uncertainties and questions. Orientation covers conventions; a new subsystem may require another reading pass.

For work with meaningful uncertainty, ask for a plan before implementation: what will be hard to verify and how, the proposed approach, and anything in the request that appears mistaken or incomplete. Answer those questions substantively. Keep this proportionate; a small, understood change may need only a short statement.

A useful brief explains what the user should be able to do or conclude, the boundaries of the assignment, relevant evidence, and the gate. Distinguish required constraints from suggested implementation. Give technical direction when useful, while leaving room to challenge it.

**Working and reporting discoveries**

Scope limits changes, not relevant investigation or reporting. Read sibling repositories and available logs when a claim depends on them. Check facts that can be checked; label assumptions that remain.

A discovery that invalidates the proposed approach or prevents the requested behaviour needs attention during the work. A defect in the implementation being delivered belongs in its acceptance discussion, even when correcting it reaches beyond the expected files.

Record useful observations when they occur with `am learn`, including evidence and a reference. Keep observations distinct from proposed rules. Tell the reviewer promptly about findings that affect the current decision.

**Verification and the gate**

Plan verification around the behaviour being claimed. A passing suite establishes its assertions. It does not establish omitted states, delivery paths or visual qualities.

For UI work, inspect the rendered result in relevant states and measure layout where geometry matters. For streamed behaviour, exercise the path the client actually consumes. Explain any gap between fixtures and real records. Confirm that test tools use the intended temporary services and files.

For a feature worked alone, run the repository’s required checks. For a batch, run appropriate checks and commit each feature separately, then run the full gate on the combined result. The named gate owner ensures that failures are resolved and the reviewed revision is the one released.

Re-run checks when changes or unresolved concerns justify it. Existing results can be used when their revision, environment and relevance are clear.

The gate includes review of the requested outcome, code, verification evidence and documentation. Required checks passing is necessary but does not settle acceptance. An acknowledged material gap needs an explicit reviewer decision.

Helpers leave pushing and deployment to the named owner unless assigned otherwise. The owner follows the repository’s release procedure and reports what was committed, pushed and deployed.

**The report and review**

A report states what changed, what was verified, what remains open, and what was noticed outside the work. Include the commit or range, whether the gate was individual or shared, and any missing evidence. Keep detail proportional to what the reviewer needs.

The reviewer compares the result with the agreed outcome, examines consequential gaps and records acceptance or a request for further work. Give unrelated findings an owner or an explicit deferral so that “noticed” does not become their final destination.

Record the run review with `am runs review`. The current cause labels—model, brief and doc—are shorthand. Explain the evidence, contributing factors and uncertainty in the note. Distinguish a changed requirement from a failure to implement the earlier one. Preserve corrections in the feature history and learnings log.

**Debriefing and preserving evidence**

After a substantial batch or surprising result, ask what helped, what was missing or over-specified, what cost unexpected effort, and what the helper withheld. Compare the raw answer with the reviewer’s account. Resolve consequential findings before forgetting the helper.

Check what the run record actually preserves. Orientation, planning, review, repairs and debriefing may lie outside its window. Preserve the relevant material or references separately.

Compare costs on stated, matching boundaries. Identify cumulative totals, missing data and interrupted measurements. Account for reviewer effort and rework when assessing the cost of obtaining an accepted result.

**Curating the practice**

`am learnings` is the observation record. Correct an entry with another entry. Put durable repository facts in repository documentation, working conventions here, and qualified model-selection judgments in `models.md`.

During curation, read the observations and enough underlying evidence to check them. Separate established facts, promising practices and unanswered questions. Look for contradictions and counterexamples. Remove or soften claims whose evidence does not support them.

A person reviews the proposed curation. Once applied, append a curation entry naming the entries considered and the revision that changed the method. Keep enough provenance that the next review can explain why a practice exists and reconsider it.
